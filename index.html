Section 01

Purpose and design intent

This dashboard exists to give the Apple UK leadership team integrated visibility over the firm's strategic and HR position in real time, without requiring them to assemble briefings from disparate functional reports.

The brief that initiated this build was direct. Several Apple UK business units are under-performing key rivals, no real-time monitoring system currently exists, and the new CEO needs an instrument that captures both day-to-day operating signals and forward-looking predictive capability. This dashboard answers that brief.

The design follows the seven Topic 8 "Good Dashboard" principles taught in BEM3063: clear and relevant metrics, customisation and flexibility, real-time data, data with instruction on how to use it, benchmarking and historical comparison, filtering, interpretation, consolidation, and predictive capability. Every design decision below traces back to one of these principles.

i
This dashboard is a decision-support instrument, not a single source of truth. Every figure is anchored to a publicly auditable source where one exists, an industry-tracked source where it does not, or clearly labelled "illustrative" where neither is available. Every tile carries a data-class badge so the user always knows which class applies.
Section 02

The three levels

The dashboard is organised into three pedagogical levels that correspond directly to the analytics typology taught in BEM3063: Descriptive (what is happening now), Diagnostic (why it is happening), and Predictive and Prescriptive (what will happen and what to do). The typology follows Davenport, Harris and Shapiro (2010).

LevelQuestion it answersTools usedBest for
L1 · DescriptiveWhat is the firm's current position?Twelve KPI tiles, sparklines, RAG indicatorsSixty-second executive scan, daily standup
L2 · DiagnosticWhy is the position what it is?Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan and Norton, 2005), four causal lensesRoot-cause discussion, weekly leadership review
L3 · PredictiveWhat will happen, and what should we do?Six modules, forecast bands, scenario sliders, simulatorsStrategic planning, pre-board off-sites

Switch between levels using the L1 · L2 · L3 · UG pill at the top of every page, or press 1, 2, or 3 on the keyboard. Filters and the role selection persist across levels so context is not lost.

Section 03

Reading a tile

Every Level 1 tile follows the same anatomy. Once the convention is learned, any tile can be read in five seconds.

Tile anatomy, top to bottom
  1. Label at top-left, the metric name in plain language. Re-labelled per role.
  2. Data-class badge on the head. AUDIT (publicly auditable), INDUSTRY (industry-tracked panel), or ILLUSTRATIVE (modelled in the absence of public disclosure).
  3. RAG dot at top-right, single colour-coded indicator. Green within target band, amber on watch, red critical.
  4. Value in large type, centre. The headline number, updated in near-real-time on the live data feed.
  5. Sub-value, the comparator. A target, a benchmark, or the rival figure, so the headline is never decontextualised.
  6. Delta pill, directional change vs prior period, with an arrow and a coloured tint.
  7. Visualisation, sparkline, donut, gauge, or comparison bar, depending on what the metric needs.
  8. Source citation at the footer, the exact data origin and currency, so any number can be audited back to filings.
  9. Updated timestamp, when the figure was last refreshed.

Click or tap any tile to open the slide-over drill-down sheet. See Section 07 for what that sheet contains.

Section 04

RAG thresholds and how they are set

RAG (Red, Amber, Green) status follows defined rules. Status is not subjective. It is set by variance against a stated target or benchmark, with override criteria for leading-indicator drift.

StatusDefault ruleOverride conditions
GreenWithin ±5% of target or benchmarkDemoted to amber if a leading indicator (cohort breakdown) is materially worse, even where the headline is on target.
Amber5 to 15% variance, or upward-trending riskEscalated to red if variance widens for two consecutive periods, or if a critical alert is open against the same business unit.
RedAbove 15% variance, or an active critical alertNo override. Red triggers SLA-clock visibility for the named owner.
!
Why some tiles look harsher than the headline number suggests. Engagement at 72/100 against a 75 target is mathematically only 4% below: green by the default rule. It is shown as amber because the trend is downward across three consecutive quarters and the retail-floor cohort has fallen to 68. The dashboard prefers to surface a softening signal early rather than mask it inside a band-tolerance that is still nominally green.
Section 05

Filters and how they compose

Four filter dimensions, namely Period, Business Unit, Geography, and Cohort, mutate the dashboard's data in real time. Filters compose. Applying multiple at once narrows the view conjunctively.

FilterOptionsWhat changes
PeriodQ · YTD · 12M · CustomTime horizon for every metric. Period-sensitive figures recompute, structural figures (gender pay gap, regulatory cases) do not.
Business UnitAll · Retail · Services · Corporate / B2B · OnlineNarrows revenue and customer figures to the chosen unit. Selecting Services amplifies the under-performance signal.
GeographyUK · England · Scotland · Wales · NIRe-scopes geo-sensitive metrics. Scotland has a narrower store-productivity spread because no London flagships sit inside it.
CohortAll staff · Retail floor · Genius · Corporate HQ · Online support · LeadershipRe-scopes people metrics. The retail-floor cohort surfaces 12.1% attrition and 68 engagement, the leading-indicator view of the headline 8.4% and 72.

Active filters are highlighted in blue on the chip bar. Click Reset at the right of the filter bar to return to the default view.

Section 06

Role views

The role pill in the top-right swaps both the order of the twelve tiles and the emphasis of selected labels and sub-values. Same data, four lenses.

CEO view

Default. Foregrounds revenue, services growth, engagement, alerts, and BU portfolio status. Designed for the daily integrative scan across strategic, financial, operational, and HR signals.

CFO view

Foregrounds £-denominated and disclosure-risk metrics. Tile 1 sub-value flips from the FY25 audited combined turnover to the gap-to-target framing. Pay-gap tile re-labels as "Pay equity, disclosure risk" with entity-level detail surfaced.

COO view

Foregrounds operational levers. Store-productivity tile rises to position one. Revenue tile reframes as "Channel revenue throughput" with the Retail and Online split surfaced.

CHRO view

Foregrounds people metrics. Engagement and attrition rise to positions one and two. Revenue tile recomputes as "Revenue per UK head". Early-tenure cohort callouts are made first-class. AI talent attrition is escalated to a top-three signal.

Section 07

The drill-down sheet

Clicking any tile opens a slide-over panel from the right. The sheet contains everything needed to interrogate the figure, including Companies House proxy disclosures and any active alerts attached to that metric.

  1. Hero value and sub-value at large size for share-screen reading.
  2. RAG with rationale, the explicit reason for the current status, not just the colour.
  3. Trend chart at a larger scale than the tile sparkline allows.
  4. Narrative, one paragraph explaining why this metric matters for the under-performing-units diagnosis.
  5. Breakdown table, the constituent figures, including data-quality flags.
  6. Active alerts or segments, for tiles that aggregate, with each underlying line listed with its owner.
  7. Source citation, the auditable origin (e.g. Apple Retail UK Ltd, Companies House 04996702, FY25 filing of 13 Apr 2026).

Close the sheet with the close button, by clicking outside it, or by pressing Escape.

Section 08

Predictive modules (Level 3)

Level 3 contains six interactive modules. Each module is paired with a methodology and limitations drawer at the bottom, a deliberate design choice, since predictive output without stated assumptions is decision-grade misinformation.

ModuleWhat it predictsMethodKey limitation
1 · Revenue ForecastUK revenue 3, 6, 12 months forwardOLS regression, lagged consumer-confidence and £/$ inputs, R² ≈ 0.71Structural break risk on Galaxy AI launch and CMA conduct requirements
2 · Attrition Risk Matrix12-month departure probability by tenure × roleLogistic regression on tenure, role, location, manager ratingPredictive is not prescriptive without intervention design (Davenport, Harris and Shapiro, 2010)
3 · Wage Inflation SimulatorUK margin under different wage and attach scenariosElasticity model: margin = base − β·Δwage + γ·ΔattachHolds revenue and FX constant. No price pass-through
4 · Galaxy S26 ImpactiOS share movement post-launchCalibrated on prior six Samsung launches, scaled by feature-overlapExcludes Pixel 10 cross-pressure
5 · CMA Conduct Cost ScenariosUK revenue at risk under three regulatory statesEU DMA cost as proxy, scaled by UK App Store revenue shareExcludes brand-equity impact
6 · Scenario SandboxComposite revenue and margin under FX × wage × Services-growth inputsMultiplicative compositeHolds competitor moves constant
!
Read the methodology drawer before quoting any L3 figure in a board paper. The forecasts are conditional projections, not guarantees. Correlation is not causation, and historical fits do not guarantee future performance, particularly through structural breaks like a major rival's AI launch or a regulator's conduct requirement.

i
Why UK revenue is shown as a two-entity proxy of £4.45bn (FY25). Apple's full UK economic activity is materially larger than the sum of the two UK-incorporated trading entities, but the residual is routed through Apple Distribution International (Ireland) and is not separately disclosed at country level. The two-entity proxy combines Apple Retail UK Ltd at £2.79bn (FY25) and Apple (UK) Ltd at £1.66bn (FY24, latest filed) for a combined £4.45bn. This is the most defensible publicly auditable UK anchor and the figure used throughout the dashboard. The Tile 1 drill-down sheet labels the excluded Distribution International stream explicitly.
Section 10

Decision workflows

Three end-to-end workflows show how the dashboard is intended to be used in live decision-making. Each follows the descriptive, diagnostic, predictive arc.

Workflow A · Daily executive scan (60 seconds)
1
Open Level 1 with default filters
Twelve tiles render in 60 seconds. Scan the RAG indicators left to right.
2
Click any red or newly-amber tile
Read the rationale, the narrative, and the source. Note the owner if alerts are attached.
3
Decide whether to escalate
If escalation is warranted, the alert SLA clock begins from the moment the tile is opened.
Workflow B · Diagnosing UK Services under-performance
1
L1, confirm the headline
Tile 3 shows UK Services growing at +6.4% YoY against US Services at +12.1%, a 5.7pp gap.
2
L2, trace the cause through the Balanced Scorecard
The chevron at the top of L2 names the bottleneck: Internal Processes to Customer. Customer panel shows 2.4× attach-rate dispersion across stores. Learning and Growth panel shows 18.2% sub-12-month-tenure attrition.
3
L3, quantify the lever
Module 3 (wage and attach simulator): a 2pp Services-attach uplift recovers 0.4pp of operating margin. Module 6 (sandbox): the same uplift adds 90m of FY26 revenue at constant FX.
Workflow C · Pre-board off-site planning
1
Set scenarios in L3 Module 6
Use the FX, wage, and Services-growth sliders to test bull, base, and bear cases.
2
Pair with Module 5
Toggle between Minimal, Moderate, and Full opening CMA conduct scenarios to layer regulatory cost on top of the macro outlook.
3
Read the methodology drawers
Quote any L3 figure in board papers only with its stated R², its assumptions, and its limitations. The drawers exist for exactly this purpose.

Section 12

Glossary

Selected terms used across the dashboard, defined in the sense intended in BEM3063 and Apple's UK reporting context.

TermDefinition
Attach rateProportion of hardware purchases that result in a Services subscription within a defined window. The UK and US gap in this metric drives the under-performing-units diagnosis.
Balanced ScorecardA four-lens performance framework (Kaplan and Norton, 2005) that links Learning and Growth, then Internal Processes, then Customer, then Financial outcomes as a causal chain.
BSC bottleneckThe point in the causal chain at which the firm's performance breaks. Apple UK's bottleneck is between Internal Processes and Customer.
CMA SMSThe Competition and Markets Authority's Strategic Market Status designation, granted to Apple's mobile platform on 22 October 2025 under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024.
Glassdoor proxyUse of public Glassdoor UK ratings as a triangulating signal alongside the internal pulse-survey composite.
Leading indicatorA metric that moves before the headline figure does. The retail-floor cohort engagement at 68 is a leading indicator of the headline 72 falling further.
RAGRed, Amber, Green status indicator. See Section 04 for the threshold rules.
SLA clockService Level Agreement timer. Begins the moment a critical alert is opened, and tracks against the named owner's resolution commitment.
Two-entity proxyThe disclosed-turnover figure built from Apple Retail UK Ltd plus Apple (UK) Ltd, combined £4.45bn. Excludes UK sales routed through Apple Distribution International (Ireland). See Section 09.
